Card games to play offline on a flight

A plane gives you a small tray table, a seat you can't leave much and, often, no Wi-Fi. Some card games suit that well and some don't. This guide sorts them: games for a deck of cards, games for a phone or tablet in airplane mode, and a few to leave for the ground.

Airplane Games Studio, which publishes this site, makes one of the games below, Airplane Rummy. It is listed with its limits, like the others.

What suits a plane

  • Cards held in the hand. A tray table has room for a pile or two, not a wide spread of cards.
  • Games you can stop. Meals, drinks and turbulence interrupt, so short hands or a game that saves itself work well.
  • Quiet games. Games of speed and slapping are hard to play in a seat and loud for the people around you.

With a deck of cards

For two players, each of these needs one ordinary 52-card deck.

Gin rummy

Each player is dealt ten cards and tries to arrange them into sets of one rank and runs in one suit. On your turn you draw one card and discard one. When your unmatched cards add up to 10 points or less, you can knock to end the hand, and with none left over you go gin for a bonus. Everything stays in your hand except two piles, and a hand is short, so it is easy to stop between hands.

Crazy Eights

Two players get seven cards each, and more players get five. On your turn, play a card that matches the top of the discard pile in number or in suit, or draw from the stock. An 8 can go on anything, and whoever plays it names the next suit. The first to run out of cards wins the hand. It is quick to learn and needs only one pile.

Cribbage

Two players get six cards each, put two into a spare hand called the crib, then score for pairs, runs and cards that add up to 15. The first to 121 points wins. It is usually scored on a board with pegs, and a small travel board fits a tray table. A pencil and paper work too.

Go Fish

Players ask each other for a rank they already hold, such as "any 7s?", and collect sets of four. A wrong guess means "go fish": draw from the stock. It works for young children. Two can play, though it goes better with three to six. Keep your voices down.

War

The deck is split in two, and each player turns up a card at once: the higher card takes both. There are no decisions to make, so it suits young children. The game goes on until one player has all the cards.

Games to leave for the ground

  • Speed and Spit. Both players play at the same time, as fast as they can, onto shared piles. Cards fly, and it gets loud.
  • Solitaire with real cards. Klondike, the usual solitaire, lays out seven columns side by side, which is a squeeze on a tray table. On a flight, a solitaire game on a phone is easier.
  • Games usually played by four, such as Hearts and Spades. They need four players around one surface, and a row of seats facing forward makes that awkward.

On a phone or tablet

Some card games on phones need a connection, for an account, for ads or to play against other people, and don't work fully in airplane mode. Check yours before you fly:

  1. Install the game at home and open it once.
  2. Turn on airplane mode, and check that Wi-Fi is off too: Wi-Fi can stay on in airplane mode.
  3. Play a full round or hand.
  4. Close the game completely, open it again while still offline, and check that it starts.
  5. Charge your phone, and bring a cable or a battery in case your seat has no power.

Airplane Rummy

Airplane Rummy: Five Suits is our game: rummy with a fifth suit, planes, for you against 1 to 6 computer players on iPhone and iPad.

  • What suits a flight: it works in airplane mode, and fully when started offline. It has no ads, no account and no timer. A match is about fifteen to twenty minutes and saves as you play, so you can close it for landing and pick up where you stopped.
  • What doesn't: you can't play it with the person next to you, because every other seat is a computer player. It isn't available on Android.

It is free on the App Store for iPhone and iPad with iOS 15 or later. More about the game, with screenshots: Airplane Rummy: Five Suits.

What to pack

  • A deck of cards in its box, so it doesn't spread through your bag.
  • A pencil, and a printed rummy score sheet if you play rummy.
  • Your phone or tablet, charged, with its games checked in airplane mode.
