Rummy rules and variants

Rummy is a family of card games, not one game. They share a core: draw a card, discard a card, and collect your cards into sets and runs. The details change from game to game and from table to table. This page explains the core in plain words, then what varies, and how Airplane Rummy plays it.

The core, in short

  • Each player is dealt a hand. The rest of the deck is the stock, face down, and one card turned face up starts the discard pile.
  • On your turn, draw one card, from the stock or from the top of the discard pile, then discard one card.
  • Collect your cards into melds: sets of one rank and runs in one suit.
  • Go out by getting rid of your cards, or by having every card in a meld. The others count what they still hold.

Sets and runs

Sets and runs are both called melds. Each needs at least three cards.

  • A set, also called a group or a book, is three or more cards of the same rank, such as three 7s. With one deck, a set has at most four cards.
  • A run, also called a sequence, is three or more cards of one suit in order, such as the 4, 5 and 6 of hearts.

A card counts in one meld at a time. In the usual rules aces are low, so ace, 2, 3 is a run and Queen, King, ace isn't. Some tables count aces high as well, and a few let a run turn the corner from King to ace to 2.

A turn

  1. Draw. Take the top card of the stock, which you see only once it is yours, or the top card of the discard pile, which everyone can see.
  2. Meld, in games that lay melds on the table. Put a set or run face up in front of you. Basic rummy also lets you lay off: add a card from your hand to a meld already on the table.
  3. Discard. Put one card face up on the discard pile. In the basic game you can't take the top discard and put the same card straight back.

Going out and scoring

In basic rummy you go out when you get rid of your last card, by melding, laying it off or discarding it, and play stops at once. The others then count the cards left in their hands. In the usual count, Kings, Queens and Jacks are worth 10, aces 1 and the other cards their number.

Games keep score in one of two ways:

  • Penalty points: the cards left in each hand count against that player, and the lowest total wins.
  • Points to the winner: whoever went out scores the cards left in the other hands, and the game is played to a target score.

Some games add a third way: you score for the cards you meld and lose points for the cards still in your hand.

A few variants

Gin rummy

Two players, ten cards each, and melds stay in your hand. You end a hand by knocking when your unmatched cards, called deadwood, add up to 10 points or less. With no deadwood at all you go gin and score a bonus. Unless you went gin, the other player can then lay off cards on your melds.

500 rummy

Also called 500 rum. You score for the cards you meld and lose points for the cards left in your hand, and the game ends when someone reaches 500. You may take a card from deep in the discard pile, as long as you take every card above it too and use that card in a meld.

Contract rummy

A series of deals, each with a contract: a set requirement your first meld must meet, such as a certain number of sets or runs. The contracts usually get harder from deal to deal.

Rounds that grow

Some rummy games deal a different number of cards each round. In some, the hand grows by one card a round and the wild rank changes to match. Airplane Rummy is one of them.

Rearranging the table

In some rummy games, when you meld you may also rearrange the melds already on the table to make new ones with cards from your hand.

Words you'll meet

  • Meld: a set or a run.
  • Stock: the face-down cards you draw from.
  • Discard pile: the face-up pile you discard onto.
  • Lay off: add a card to a meld already on the table.
  • Deadwood: cards that aren't in any meld.
  • Knock: in gin rummy, end the hand with 10 points of deadwood or less.
  • Go out: finish your hand and end the round.
  • Wild card: a card that can stand in for any card in a meld.
