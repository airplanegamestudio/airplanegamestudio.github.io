How to play Airplane Rummy

Airplane Rummy is rummy with a fifth suit, planes. These are its rules as the game plays them on iPhone and iPad, where you play against 1 to 6 computer players, offline.

The short version

  • A match is eleven rounds. Round 1 deals 3 cards to each player, and each round deals one more, up to 13.
  • The rank that matches the number of cards dealt is wild that round. Jokers are always wild.
  • On your turn, draw one card, arrange your hand, then discard one card.
  • When every card you keep is in a set or a run, you can go out. Everyone else then gets one final turn.
  • Cards left outside your sets and runs count against you. The lowest total after round 11 wins.

The cards

The deck is two packs of 58 cards, 116 in all. Each pack has five suits (hearts, clubs, spades, diamonds and planes), each running 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, Jack, Queen and King, and each pack adds three Jokers. There are no aces or twos.

With two packs, every card is in the deck twice, so you might hold both 9s of planes.

Players

2 to 7 players: you and 1 to 6 computer players. The table seats five until you pick another number, and the game remembers your pick. To change it, open Rules and settings and start a new match. The computer players see their own cards and the discard pile, never your hand.

Eleven rounds

Every round deals one more card than the last. After the deal, one card is turned face up to start the discard pile, and the rest of the deck waits face down. A random player starts round 1, and each later round starts with the next player round the table.

Sets and runs

Sets and runs are called melds. Each needs at least three cards.

  • A set is three or more cards of one rank, in any suits: the 9 of hearts, the 9 of clubs and the 9 of planes, for example. A set can hold both copies of the same card.
  • A run is three or more cards of one suit in order, such as the 4, 5 and 6 of hearts. It can go low to high or high to low. Runs stop at 3 and at King and never wrap around, so a run is at most eleven cards long.

Keep the cards of each meld side by side in your hand. The game reads your hand from the left, each meld keeps going for as long as the next card fits it, and a card counts in one meld only. As you arrange, the game marks each meld it counts, so you can check before you discard.

Your cards arrive sorted low to high, with any wild cards at the right end. A card you draw also goes at the right end, and the game never re-sorts your hand, so your arrangement stays as you left it.

Wild cards

Each round, the rank that matches the number of cards dealt is wild, in every suit: all the 3s in round 1, all the 4s in round 2, and so on up to the Kings in round 11. The six Jokers are wild in every round.

A wild card can take the place of any card in a set or a run, at either end or in the middle. A meld can hold more than one wild, and three or more wild cards together make a set.

Wilds only help inside a meld. A wild card still loose when the round ends costs you 20 points, and a loose Joker costs 50.

Your turn

  1. Draw. Take the face-up card from the top of the discard pile, or the top card of the deck.
  2. Arrange. Drag cards to put your sets and runs together. Undo puts back your last move.
  3. Discard. Tap the card you want to give up, then tap Discard, or drag it onto the pile. If every card you keep fits a meld, you can tap Go out instead.

There is no timer, so take each turn at your own pace. The player after you can take the card you discard.

Going out and the final turn

You can go out when, after your draw, every card but one fits into sets and runs. Tap that one card, then tap Go out. Going out is always your choice: Discard keeps the round going instead.

The cards you keep are always the number dealt that round. In round 6 you keep 8, so you need one meld of 8, a 5 and a 3, or two 4s. Before each of the first seven rounds, the game lists every way to go out.

When someone goes out, each other player gets one final turn, in order. Then the round ends and every hand is scored.

Scoring

When a round ends, cards in sets and runs score nothing. Each loose card counts against you:

A card of the wild rank counts 20 whatever its number: in round 9, a loose Jack costs 20, not 11.

Whoever went out scores 0 for the round. Each round's points add to your total, and after round 11 the lowest total wins. Players on the same total share a place.

After each round, the score sheet shows every meld, every loose card and how each total adds up, such as 115 + 27 = 142.

One round, played through

Round 4 at a table of three: you, Mira and Orion. Each player gets six cards, so every 6 is wild.

The deal

Your six cards are the 8 and 9 of hearts, the Jack of clubs, the Queen of spades, the King of diamonds and the 6 of planes. The 6 of planes is wild this round, so it sits at the right end.

Your first turn

The face-up card is the 10 of hearts. Take it and move it beside the 8 and 9: the 8, 9 and 10 of hearts make a run. Then discard the King of diamonds. Nothing in your hand goes with it, and it would cost 13 points if you still held it at the end of the round. Mira and Orion each take a turn.

Your second turn

You draw the Jack of spades from the deck. With the Jack of clubs and the wild 6 of planes, it makes a set of three Jacks. Move the Queen of spades to the right end, so your hand reads: 8, 9 and 10 of hearts, Jack of clubs, 6 of planes, Jack of spades, Queen of spades. Tap the Queen, then tap Go out. The six cards you keep are a run and a set, so you score 0 this round.

Where the wild sits matters. Had your hand read 8, 9 and 10 of hearts, 6 of planes, Jack of clubs, Jack of spades, the wild would have counted as the Jack of hearts in a run of four. The two real Jacks would be loose, 22 points, and you couldn't go out.

The final turns

Mira and Orion each get one final turn, then the round is scored. Mira is left with one loose card, the 4 of diamonds: 4 points. Orion is left with three, the King of hearts, the Queen of diamonds and the 3 of spades: 13 + 12 + 3 = 28 points.

If the totals after round 3 were you 21, Mira 30 and Orion 18, the score sheet now shows:

  • You: 21 + 0 = 21
  • Mira: 30 + 4 = 34
  • Orion: 18 + 28 = 46

You lead with the lowest total, with seven rounds to go.

Questions

Do I have to go out as soon as I can?

No. Go out is a separate choice from Discard, so you decide when.

Can a meld be only wild cards?

Yes. Three or more wild cards together count as a set.

Does a run wrap around from King to 3?

No. A run stops at King at the top and at 3 at the bottom.

What happens on my final turn when someone else went out?

You draw and discard as usual, and then your hand is scored. If every card you keep is in a meld by then, you score 0 too.

What if the deck runs out?

The discard pile, all but its top card, is shuffled to make a new deck, and play goes on.

Can I play against other people?

No. Every other seat is a computer player, and the whole game runs on your iPhone or iPad, offline.

Can I stop in the middle of a match?

Yes. The game saves as you play, so you can close it and pick up where you stopped.
