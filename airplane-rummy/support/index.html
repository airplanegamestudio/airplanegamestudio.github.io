Airplane Rummy support

Airplane Rummy is a five-suit rummy game for iPhone, iPad, and Android phones and tablets. There is no account, no ads and nothing to buy, and the whole game works without a connection.

Quick answers

Continue a match. Open the app. The match in progress resumes where you stopped.

Start a new match. Tap the book icon at the top of the table, then START A NEW MATCH. Pick how many play, from two to seven, then START NEW. This replaces the match in progress. There is no other copy of it.

Change how many play. The choice is on the round-1 card before your first deal, on the new-match screen (book icon, then START A NEW MATCH) and under the standings when a match ends. Pick two to seven. The table seats five until you choose another size, and the app remembers your choice. A match in progress keeps its size; the Rules and settings sheet shows how many are at the table now.

Turn sound off or on. Tap the book icon, then the Sound switch. The app remembers your choice. How loud is set by your phone's volume buttons.

Learn the rules. Tap the book icon. The rules are at the top of that sheet, and SHOW QUICK GUIDE replays the four-page introduction.

Play without a connection. Everything works in airplane mode. The app never needs a connection: it works fully when started offline. Where a U.S. state law requires an age check, your phone may go online to answer Apple's or Google's age-range question; the game itself sends nothing and keeps no answer. The privacy policy explains it.

A save will not open. Close the app fully and open it again. If you still see a recovery message and want to keep the old match, email us before starting a new one.

The game in brief

Two decks, five suits (Hearts, Clubs, Spades, Diamonds and Planes), six Jokers, 116 cards. Eleven rounds, dealing three cards up to thirteen. The rank that matches the hand size is wild that round, and Jokers are always wild. Draw, arrange sets and runs, discard. Go out when every card you keep fits a meld; everyone else gets one final turn. Unmatched cards count against you, and the lowest total after eleven rounds wins. Two to seven players, chosen when you start a new match; five unless you choose another size. The computer players see only the discard pile and their own cards.
